# Finding value: what a statement is worth, and how to put a number on it

This document researches one question — what makes a finding valuable — and proposes a way to quantify it. It is the research deliverable of issue #353. The digest ranks statements, and the two ranking measures tested so far have failed as the whole rule: compression gain per character separates the must tier from the should tier at AUC 0.552 within datasets, and action-class membership does not separate them at all (the interestingness study, `docs/benchmarks/2026-11-17-interestingness-study.md`). This document goes deeper on the value side of that problem: how value has been quantified in decision theory, in knowledge discovery, in visualization research, and in the insight-recommendation systems that ship today, and what number a profiler can defensibly put on a statement.

The proposal, stated here and developed in section 4: a statement's value is an expected value of information, computed against a stated reference decision problem, as a product of four terms —

value(statement) = reach × effect × lever × link

— where reach and effect are measured from the data and the report, and lever and link are read from a catalog of actions whose default weights are argued, ranged, overridable at runtime, and conditioned on declared purpose. A sibling measurement (issue #347) is pricing the per-family data-action effects over the gate's labeled statements; this document designs the slot those measurements fill and does not invent their results. No library code changes under this document.

## 1. The question

The study established three facts that frame everything here.

First, every finding type licenses at least one action, so actionability as a yes/no property separates nothing. What varies is which action, and what the action is worth.

Second, compression prices a statement by how much it surprises a distributional background. The statements whose worth is least distributional — the refusals, which state that a column, a value, or a period must not enter the analysis as it stands — price at exactly zero bits, because a sentinel code is a legitimate mass point of the marginal distribution. A measure that prices the most protective statements at zero is measuring the wrong quantity.

Third, an arithmetic combination of the failed measures was rejected in the study because no measurement supplied an exchange rate between, say, a refusal and a driver statement. Any single number that ranks statements across families asserts such an exchange rate somewhere. The question is therefore not whether to have one, but where to put it so that it is visible, arguable, and falsifiable.

The direction recorded on issue #347, and the framing this document is built on: a finding's value is the value of its information, assuming the reader acts on it. The chain is statement → data action → changed artifact (a cleaned dataset, a fitted model, a reported number) → business action. Only the first links are measurable from the data. The last link — whether the changed artifact changes a decision — is where most of the value sits and where the data are silent. A quantification that ignores the last link prices surprise or effect size and calls it value. A quantification that models it has to estimate the probability that the chain is actually traversed, and has to say, per action, what decision the chain ends at.

## 2. The headline answer

What makes an insight an insight, on the evidence assembled in section 3, is not a property of the pattern in the data. It is a property of the pattern's position in a chain that ends at a decision:

- Decision theory gives the exact statement. Information has value exactly insofar as it can change the decision that would otherwise be taken, and its value is the expected improvement in the outcome of that decision. Information that cannot change any available decision has value exactly zero, whatever its statistical strength.
- The knowledge-discovery literature named the same property "actionability" thirty years ago and never turned it into a validated number. The one branch that did put a number on it — profit-scored pattern and model evaluation — priced the artifact by the profit of the action it triggers, and handled unknown business parameters by integrating over a distribution for them rather than by guessing a point value.
- Visualization research defines insight as, among other things, "relevant": connected to domain knowledge and ending in domain impact. Its measurement tradition counts insights in protocols and codes them by hand; it has no scalar, and its own follow-up work shows practitioners gloss "relevant" as "actionable, tied to the goals of the organization."
- Every insight-scoring system that ships computes some form of effect size times statistical evidence. None includes a term for whether the finding reaches a decision, and none has been calibrated against labeled judgments of value.

So the number proposed here is an expected value with the traversal made explicit. The measured effect of a statement is multiplied by the probability that the chain from the statement to a business action is actually traversed, and by a lever term that is zero when no decision exists whose alternatives differ over the statement's object. A finding nobody will act on scores zero however large its measured effect. A finding about something the decision-maker cannot change scores zero however decision-relevant it looks in the abstract. Those two zeros are not defects of the formula; they are the two best-established facts in the literature this document surveys, and any candidate measure that cannot produce them is measuring something else.

## 3. Research

Each source is cited for what this work takes from it and what it rejects. Bibliographic details were verified by fetching the source or a faithful scholarly record on 2026-11-17, except where marked.

### 3.1 Value of information: the ceiling, and what it assumes

**Howard, "Information Value Theory," IEEE Transactions on Systems Science and Cybernetics 2(1): 22–26, 1966; and the formal development in Poh & Horvitz, "A Graph-Theoretic Analysis of Information Value" (1996, authors' manuscript, Microsoft Research; venue not verified).**

The definitions, as Poh & Horvitz state them. A decision problem has a set of alternative actions, a set of uncertain states with probabilities, and a utility over action–state pairs. The expected value of perfect information on a variable X before action A is the difference between the certain equivalent of deciding with X known and the certain equivalent of deciding without it: EVPI = ce(M with X observed) − ce(M). Three properties follow immediately and matter here.

1. EVPI is never negative: free information, used, cannot degrade the decision (their Lemma 1 and Lemma 2).
2. EVPI is exactly zero when the same action is optimal under every state of X. Information has no value unless it has the potential to change the decision. The teaching literature states the same point in one line: if the decision maker will make the same decision regardless of what the new information says, the information has no value.
3. The net value subtracts the cost of acquiring the information (NEVPI = EVPI − Cost). A briefing's analogue of cost is the reader's characters and attention, which is why the study's pay-for-your-characters discipline survives in section 4 as a shadow price rather than as the objective.

Poh & Horvitz add a fourth result that this design uses directly: in an influence diagram, the value of information on a variable attenuates with the variable's distance from the value node, and a variable with no path to the value node has EVPI of zero. Value decays along the chain between the information and the decision. The statement → action → artifact → decision chain of issue #353 is that chain, and the link term in section 4 is its attenuation factor.

What the theory assumes: a named decision, named alternatives, a probability distribution, and a utility. A profiler in bare mode has none of the four for the reader's real decisions. What it can supply is a reference decision problem — the generic analyst's next steps with this dataset (the data and modeling actions of issue #347's layers 1 and 2), extended by whatever decisions a declared purpose names (layer 3). The number the profiler computes is therefore an expected value of sample information under the reference problem, not the reader's true value of information. EVPI also prices *perfect* information; a profiler's statement is an imperfect signal, so the correct concept is the value of the signal actually delivered, with EVPI as its ceiling. Both qualifications are stated on the number wherever it is used: it is conditional on the reference problem, and it is conditional on the reader acting.

*Taken:* the definition, the two zeros (no decision change, no path to a decision), the attenuation along the chain, and the net-of-cost form. *Rejected:* nothing in the theory; what is rejected is any presentation of the profiler's number as the reader's value of information rather than as an expected value under a declared reference problem.

### 3.2 Actionability in knowledge discovery: a criterion that became a process, and the profit-scored exception

**Silberschatz & Tuzhilin (1996), covered at survey depth in the study; the operationalization record is what matters here.** Their actionability criterion — a pattern is interesting if the user can act on it to their advantage — was proposed as a judgment against the user's goals, and neither they nor the survey literature (Geng & Hamilton 2006) turned it into a computable scalar. The study's mapping test shows why a binary version cannot work. The record of what happened instead is in the two lines below.

**Cao & Zhang, "Domain-Driven Actionable Knowledge Discovery in the Real World," PAKDD 2006, LNCS 3918: 821–830; Cao, Zhao, Zhang, Luo, Zhang & Park, "Flexible Frameworks for Actionable Knowledge Discovery," IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering 22(9), 2010.** Cao's program splits interestingness into technical interestingness (computable from the data) and business interestingness, and is explicit about what the second is made of: business interests "may be instantiated into specific social and economic measures in terms of a problem domain. For instance, profit, return and ROI are usually used." The actionable-knowledge-discovery frameworks then aim at a process outcome — patterns converted into "operable business rules that can be seamlessly associated or integrated with business processes and systems" — through constraints, domain knowledge, and human–mining cooperation. *Taken:* the two-part split itself, which is the measured factor and the linkage factor of issue #353 under earlier names; and the insistence that the business side be instantiated in domain measures, not left as a label. *Rejected:* the resolution. Twenty years of this literature produced frameworks and processes, not a validated per-pattern number; the conversion to actions is left to domain experts in the loop. A profiler that runs unattended cannot adopt the process answer. It has to commit to the number the process literature avoided, with the avoidance recorded as a warning about what the number rests on.

**Verbeke et al.'s maximum-profit criterion, and Verbraken, Verbeke & Baesens, "A Novel Profit Maximizing Metric for Measuring Classification Performance of Customer Churn Prediction Models," IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering 25(5): 961–973, 2013 (DOI 10.1109/TKDE.2012.50); Verbraken, Bravo, Weber & Baesens, European Journal of Operational Research 238(2): 505–513, 2014.** This is the closest existing precedent for pricing a finding by its downstream action. The framework prices a churn model by the profit of the retention campaign it triggers: profit depends on customer lifetime value, the cost of contacting a customer, the cost of the retention offer, and the probability that a would-be churner accepts the offer and stays. The expected maximum profit (EMP) measure then does the thing this design copies: the acceptance probability is not known, so instead of fixing it, EMP integrates the maximum profit over a Beta distribution for it, and the remaining parameters are set to values argued from the application domain (in the published churn implementation: lifetime value 200, contact cost 10, offer cost 1, Beta(6, 14)). The empirical result that justifies the whole approach: rankings by EMP and rankings by AUC disagree materially, and selecting models by AUC produces lower realized profit. A value-blind statistical measure and a value-priced measure do not merely differ in emphasis; they choose different artifacts, and the value-priced choice is worth more money. *Taken:* three mechanics — price the artifact by the action it triggers; put a distribution, not a point, on the business parameters that cannot be measured, and report sensitivity across it; argue the default parameter values from domain practice, in writing. *Rejected:* the setting. EMP prices one artifact type (a classifier) against one named campaign with a known profit structure. A profiler prices many statement types against decisions it mostly cannot name. The mechanics transfer; the profit units do not.

### 3.3 Insight research: "relevant" is the linkage factor, and value is realized downstream

**North, "Toward Measuring Visualization Insight," IEEE Computer Graphics and Applications 26(3), 2006.** North characterizes insight as complex, deep, qualitative, unexpected, and relevant, where relevant means "deeply embedded in the data domain, connecting the data to existing domain knowledge and giving it relevant meaning" — in the fuller statement, going "beyond dry data analysis, to relevant domain impact." His proposed evaluation counts the insights users produce and characterizes them; the follow-up literature (Yi et al., BELIV 2008) records that no commonly accepted definition emerged and that the characterization resists direct measurement — insights are identified by protocol analysis and human coding. A later interview study of professional visualization users ("What are Data Insights to Professional Visualization Users?", arXiv:2008.13057) found practitioners describe insights with seven characteristics, actionable among them, and that what North calls relevant is glossed in practice as findings tied to the goals of the organization. *Taken:* the field's own definition already contains the linkage factor — "relevant" is the business-linkage weight, named in 2006 and never since quantified; and the interview evidence that practitioners mean actionability by it. *Rejected:* counting as measurement. A count of insights, coded by hand, cannot rank statements inside a briefing, and the tradition offers no scalar to borrow.

**Sacha, Stoffel, Stoffel, Kwon, Ellis & Keim, "Knowledge Generation Model for Visual Analytics," IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics 20(12): 1604–1613, 2014.** The model separates findings (what the system shows), insights (what the analyst takes from them, possibly on weak evidence), and knowledge (what survives a verification loop), in three loops: exploration, verification, knowledge generation. Two consequences for pricing. First, a profiler's statement is a finding, not yet an insight and not yet knowledge; its value is realized, if at all, in loops the profiler does not run — which is the structural reason the traversal probability belongs in the formula and why it is usually well below 1. Second, the verification loop is where escalate-family statements live: their value is the value of the verification they trigger, which is the divergence between what the analysis concludes under each resolution of the open question — the proxy already recorded for that family on issue #347. *Taken:* the finding/insight/knowledge separation and the placement of value downstream of the statement. *Rejected:* the model is descriptive. It prices nothing and ranks nothing.

### 3.4 The scores that systems actually ship: effect times evidence, no traversal term

The automated-insight literature is the only place where numbers are routinely put on findings at scale. The numbers share one structure: an effect-size term times an evidence term. None contains a traversal or linkage term, and none has been calibrated against labeled judgments of value.

**QuickInsights — Ding, Han, Xu, Zhang & Zhang, "QuickInsights: Quick and Automatic Discovery of Insights from Multi-Dimensional Data," SIGMOD 2019 (DOI 10.1145/3299869.3314037); shipped in Microsoft Power BI.** Each insight is scored as score = impact × significance. Impact is the importance of the insight's subject against the whole dataset: for each candidate impact-measure, the subject's monotone-aggregated share of the whole, maximized over impact-measures; it is bounded in [0, 1], anti-monotonic in the subject (a superset subject scores at least as much), and the impact-measures are user-specifiable "meaningful measures" — count, or a business measure such as sales. Significance is a hypothesis test of the insight's aggregate values against an insight-type-dependent null hypothesis that "reflects common situations formed up by majority of non-insights," also in [0, 1]. Evaluation: quantitative experiments on 447 real datasets covering mining effectiveness and efficiency, and qualitative user studies reporting the insights useful to expert and non-expert users. *Taken:* impact is the one term in this literature that points downstream — a share of a business measure is a crude reach measure, and making the measure user-specifiable is the declared-purpose channel in embryo. Section 4's reach term is impact generalized from "share of one measure" to "share of the analysis that flows through the statement's objects." *Rejected:* the product as a value. Impact times significance is effect times evidence. It ranks a large, well-attested triviality above a small fact that would change a decision, and its validation never compares the score against anyone's judgment of which insights were worth having.

**SeeDB — Vartak, Rahman, Madden, Parameswaran & Polyzotis, "SeeDB: Efficient Data-Driven Visualization Recommendations to Support Visual Analytics," Proceedings of the VLDB Endowment 8(13): 2182–2193, 2015.** Utility is a distance (earth mover's, Kullback–Leibler, Jensen–Shannon, or Euclidean) between the distribution a view shows on a target subset and the same view on a reference set. The paper is candid that deviation is adopted "as a first step" and that the generalized utility metric is future work. *Taken:* the reference-distribution form — a statement's effect measured against what the same computation shows elsewhere — which is the shape of several per-family proxies in the sibling measurement. *Rejected:* deviation as value. A deviation is a surprise measure with a better reference class; it prices the bank-marketing sentinel refusal no better than compression does, because the refusal is not a deviation.

**Foresight — Demiralp, Haas, Parthasarathy & Pedapati, "Foresight: Recommending Visual Insights," Proceedings of the VLDB Endowment 10(12), 2017.** An insight is "a strong manifestation of a statistical property," and each insight class carries its own strength metric — variance for dispersion, the standardized skewness coefficient for skew, kurtosis for heavy tails, the Pearson coefficient for linear correlation — used to rank instances within the class and present the top k. There is deliberately no score across classes. *Taken:* the within-class discipline, which section 4 keeps (statements compete on value per character inside the study's lexicographic steps, not in one global pool). *Rejected, as a precedent for ranking:* refusing the cross-class comparison is not available to a briefing, which must choose across classes under one character budget. Foresight shows the cost of the refusal is real — every cross-class number asserts an exchange rate — and that the field has mostly chosen not to pay it. This design pays it in one named place (the per-family normalization of section 4.1) rather than in several hidden ones.

**Calliope — Shi, Xu, Sun, Shi & Cao, "Calliope: Automatic Visual Data Story Generation from a Spreadsheet," IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics 27(2), 2021.** A fact's importance is its information quantity (self-information) times its pattern significance, and a logic-oriented Monte Carlo tree search assembles facts into a story maximizing the accumulated score. Evaluation: three example stories, two controlled experiments, and interviews with ten domain experts, measuring the efficiency and quality of story generation. *Taken:* nothing structural beyond confirmation of the effect-times-evidence pattern in a second system. *Rejected:* self-information as the effect term — it is the compression measure that the gate measurements already refuted as a ranking basis for this library.

**DataShot — Wang, Sun, Zhang, Cui, Xu, Ma & Zhang, "DataShot: Automatic Generation of Fact Sheets from Tabular Data," IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics 26(1): 895–905, 2020.** Facts are extracted with importance scores over columns and rows, composed into topics, and rendered as fact sheets; evaluation is an in-lab user study of the generated sheets. **Power BI Key Influencers (Microsoft product documentation, learn.microsoft.com).** Factors are ranked by how much they move a chosen metric's likelihood, computed with ML.NET regression models, with driver segments found by decision-tree analysis; the documentation describes the method, and no calibration of the ranking against labeled judgments of value is published with it. Both are recorded here for the same reason: they are what "actionable insight" scoring means in shipped products — a within-analysis importance ranking, validated, if at all, by user satisfaction. Neither asks whether the ranked factor reaches a decision.

**VisEval — Chen, Zhang, Xu, Ren & Yang, "VisEval: A Benchmark for Data Visualization in the Era of Large Language Models," arXiv:2407.00981, 2024** scores generated visualizations on validity, legality, and readability. It is included to mark a boundary: it prices the rendering of a chart, not the worth of a finding, and nothing in it transfers.

### 3.5 Pricing joint contributions: the marginal-contribution method and its axioms

**Ghorbani & Zou, "Data Shapley: Equitably Valuing Data for Machine Learning," ICML 2019 (arXiv:1904.02868).** The value of a training point is its average marginal contribution to a performance score, averaged over subsets of the remaining points. The Shapley form is forced by three equity conditions, of which the first matters most here: a point that changes the performance of no subset gets value zero. The second fact that matters: the value is defined only relative to a named learning algorithm and a named performance metric — the authors are explicit that there is no context-free value of a datum, and that this dependence is desirable. Both facts transfer to statements. The study left one component non-operational: the marginal gain of a statement inside a cluster (the collapse trio's joint gain of 970.2 bits against 680.8 for the sum of standalone gains). The Shapley construction is the standard answer to exactly that problem — price each member by its average marginal contribution over orderings — and the null condition is the same zero the lever term asserts, one level down: a statement that changes no analysis in any company it keeps is worth zero. *Taken:* the attribution method for clusters, and the null axiom as a design constraint. *Rejected:* the object being priced. Data Shapley prices contribution to a model's score under a fixed performance functional. A briefing has no single functional; the effect term here is per-family, and the Shapley averaging is used only inside the effect term, for clusters, not as the value itself. The computation is also exponential in principle and sampled in practice, which caps cluster sizes.

### 3.6 Setting the weights: composite-score practice

**OECD, Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators: Methodology and User Guide, OECD Publishing, 2008 (ISBN 978-92-64-04345-9).** Composite indicators are the closest mature practice to what section 4 builds: heterogeneous quantities normalized, weighted, and aggregated into one ranking number, for policy audiences. The handbook's construction sequence treats weighting and aggregation as one designed step, done "according to the underlying theoretical framework," with correlation and compensability among components addressed explicitly, and it makes uncertainty and sensitivity analysis a required step in the construction — the robustness of the ranking is assessed against, among other things, the choice of weights and the aggregation method. Two of its warnings apply verbatim. Equal weights are not the absence of weights: assigning every component the same weight asserts that every component is equally important. And additive aggregation is compensatory: a large value on one component buys out a zero on another. That second warning decides a real design question in section 4. A traversal probability of zero must zero a statement's value; under an additive score it cannot. The multiplicative form is not a preference; it is the only common aggregation in which "nobody will act on this" and "there is no lever" are expressible.

The handbook does not say how to get the weights right, because in its domain they come from the theoretical framework and from expert processes. The two working precedents for getting defensible defaults are the ones already cited: EMP's argued parameter values with a distribution over the parameter that cannot be argued (section 3.2), and fitting to labeled judgments where labels exist — which, for this library, they do, in the gate's tiers. Section 4.4 uses both: defaults argued and ranged first, then a constrained fit against the labeled statements, with the fit required to prove it is not doing all the work.


## 4. The proposal

### 4.1 The formula and its terms

For a statement s with primary action a (the study's Appendix A mapping, with the statement-level overrides of its Appendix C) under purpose profile p:

value(s) = reach(s) × effect(s) × lever(a, p) × link(a, p)

The product is read as an expectation. effect is the size of what changes if the action is taken and the chain completes. reach is the share of the analysis that the change touches. lever and link together are the probability that the chain from the statement to a business action is traversed: lever is the structural part (a decision exists whose alternatives differ over the statement's object), link is the estimated part (given such a decision exists, the traversal completes). value is therefore the expected fraction of the statement's maximum conceivable effect that survives the chain to a changed decision, under profile p. It is dimensionless. It is not money and it is not bits. Comparisons are valid inside one profile; a value under one declared purpose is not comparable to a value under another, because the reference decision problem differs.

Each term, with its source and its units:

| Term | Definition | Source | Units |
|---|---|---|---|
| reach(s) | The share of the analysis that flows through the objects the statement names. For a measure-scoped statement, the objects' share of that measure's total; for a row-scoped statement, rows in scope over total rows; for a whole-table statement, 1. Where other statements use the statement's objects, that use counts toward reach (the object-use relation the study marked partially operational). | Measured from the report and the frame account | Fraction in [0, 1] |
| effect(s) | The size of the change the statement's action makes, under the per-family proxy of the sibling measurement (issue #347), normalized per family as below. For a statement inside a cluster, its average marginal effect over sampled orderings of the cluster (section 3.5). | Measured from the data | Fraction in [0, 1], per family |
| lever(a, p) | 1 if the profile's decision set contains a decision whose alternatives differ over a's object; 0 if no such decision exists. Recorded per catalog entry as the lever the action presumes, so that a zero is a structural fact about the entry and the profile, never a small estimate. | Catalog default, conditioned by declared purpose; overridable | {0, 1} |
| link(a, p) | The estimated probability that the chain data action → changed artifact → business action completes, conditional on the statement being read, the lever existing, and the data action being taken. | Catalog default with a stated range and basis; overridable at runtime; conditioned by declared purpose | Probability in [0, 1] |

The per-family normalization of effect is the one place where an exchange rate between families is asserted, and it is asserted in the open. Each family's proxy is expressed, where the proxy allows, as a ratio against its own quantity's scale, so that the normalization is a property of the measurement and not a second judgment:

- admit or refuse: distortion prevented over the magnitude of the quantity as naively computed — the relative error the refusal stops, capped at 1.
- partition: the share of the quantity's total variation that sits between the segments the split creates — the heterogeneity the pooled number conceals, as a fraction of the whole.
- specify: held-out loss reduction over the baseline loss — the relative gain from bending the model as directed, capped at 1.
- simplify: description removed over the description of the objects involved — the share of the involved columns' description that the removal eliminates, at zero information loss.
- identify: mass double-counted over table mass — the share of the table counted twice while two encodings are treated as two things.
- re-express: the effect of the strongest statement the re-expression makes computable, recorded as such; this is the least settled term, and the study's rule already renders most re-express statements as account content, so the term prices few lines.
- escalate: divergence between the analyses under each resolution of the open question, over the scale of the larger analysis. Where the resolutions cannot both be computed from the table — the usual case, and the reason the statement escalates — effect is recorded as unpriced, and the statement is ordered by reach × lever × link alone, marked as unpriced rather than assigned a substitute number.

Two consequences of the coordinator's framing are properties of this form, and are stated as requirements any revision must preserve. A finding nobody will act on has link 0 under the applicable profile and scores zero, however large its measured effect; an additive score cannot say this, which is the concrete reason the aggregation is multiplicative (section 3.6). And information about something the decision-maker cannot change has lever 0 and scores zero, however decision-relevant it is in the abstract — the catalog's job is to distinguish that structural zero from a weak-but-positive link, which is why lever and link are separate fields and never a single weight.

### 4.2 The catalog

The catalog's entries are the actions of the taxonomy (issue #349), at the grain of the study's seven families with their principal actions named. Each entry records: the action; the business-action classes it can reach; the lever it presumes; a default link with a range; the basis of the default, in one of three grades — *measured* (part of the weight rests on a quantity the library computes), *argued* (reasoned from the action's position in the chain, in writing, in the entry), or *judgment* (a standing estimate with no present anchor). The business-action classes are a closed set of five: correct or withdraw a number others rely on; change a decision input (a forecast, a plan, a price, a budget); change a model that feeds decisions; start, stop, or redirect an operational process; change what is collected or measured next.

Default entries, as starting points for the calibration of section 4.4 — the numbers are proposals, and the ranges are part of the proposal:

| Family and principal actions | Business classes reached | Lever presumed | Default link (range) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Admit or refuse — exclude a column, a set of values, or a set of rows; restrict a period | Correct a number others rely on | The reader controls what enters their own analysis | 0.7 (0.5–0.9) | Argued. The action sits entirely inside the analysis, so traversal to a corrected artifact is near-automatic once the analyst acts; the residual uncertainty is whether the corrected artifact feeds a decision. Reach carries how much flows through the refused object. |
| Specify — add a term, a shape, an interaction, a slice; use missingness as signal | Change a model that feeds decisions; change a decision input | The reader fits or directs the model | 0.45 (0.3–0.6) | Measured in part. The held-out gain (the effect term) is measured; the open part is whether a model feeds a decision at all, which is what declared purpose exists to state. |
| Partition — split at a break; do not pool groups or periods | Change a decision input; change a model that feeds decisions | The reader controls the grouping of their analysis | 0.4 (0.25–0.55) | Argued. One step further from the artifact than a refusal: the split changes every quantity computed on either side, but a pooled number must actually be in use for the correction to reach a decision. |
| Simplify — drop a determined or duplicate-encoded column; drop duplicate rows | Correct a number others rely on | The reader controls the dataset's shape | 0.4 (0.25–0.55) | Argued. The action is lossless and near-costless, so data-level traversal is high; the business reach is the same corrected-number class as a refusal, with the smaller stakes carried by the effect term. |
| Escalate — verify a value at the source; ask the owner; collect more data | Correct a number others rely on; start or redirect an operational process; change what is collected next | Split: the check may belong to the reader, to the data's owner, or to no one the reader can reach | 0.3 (0.15–0.5) | Judgment. The chain has an extra human link and often an external lever; where the profile names no owner and no purpose, the lever field, not the link, is where an unreachable escalation should be zeroed. |
| Identify — merge duplicate encodings; designate a key or a weight | Enables other actions; rarely reaches a business class directly | The reader controls the dataset's shape | 0.2 (0.1–0.3) | Argued. Most of an identify statement's worth is enabling — it makes other statements' actions executable — and the study already renders it as account content. The low direct link records that its value travels through other statements. |
| Re-express — parse, reshape, or reformat a column into computable form | Enables other actions | The reader controls the dataset's shape | 0.2 (0.1–0.3) | Argued, on the same grounds as identify. |

Declared purpose conditions the catalog in two ways. A purpose that names decisions sets lever per entry against those decisions — this is the only route by which an escalate action whose lever belongs to an external owner acquires lever 1, and the only route by which a data action loses it (a purpose that fixes the analysis's shape can set lever 0 for actions outside it). A purpose that names the artifact's consumers adjusts link within its range. In bare mode the default profile presumes the generic analyst's levers over the dataset itself — every data and modeling action has lever 1, because the analyst can always exclude, split, and refit — and presumes no world lever, so escalate entries price on their link alone with the split lever recorded. Runtime override replaces any default per action, and an override is carried in the report's declared state like a hint, so a reader can see which weights priced the briefing they received.

### 4.3 Composition with the study's rule

The study's lexicographic rule stands; value replaces the efficiency measure inside it, and one of its steps is absorbed.

1. Refusals first — unchanged, and now with its justification stated in value terms. The values of all other statements are computed on the assumption that refusals are heeded: a driver statement's effect is the effect of the corrected quantity, not of the quantity computed over a sentinel code. A refusal that qualifies an object another statement uses is therefore a precondition of those statements' values, not a competitor with them, and no product of terms can express that. It stays lexicographic.
2. Analysis-changing statements (partition, specify, simplify) fill by value per character, replacing marginal gain per character. The pay-for-your-characters discipline is retained as the net-value move of section 3.1: the budget makes characters scarce, and greedy fill by value per character is the operational form of value net of the cost of the reader's attention.
3. Escalate statements follow, by the same efficiency, with rate-only facts still rendered by the frame account rather than as finding lines.
4. Bookkeeping (identify, re-express) renders as account content, with the study's two exceptions unchanged; where an exception renders a line, it is priced by the formula like any other line.
5. Action-free statements never render — unchanged, and consistent with the null condition of section 3.5.

The study's declared-purpose extension — a purpose that names an object promotes the escalate statements about that object — is no longer a special rule. Under the formula, a purpose changes lever and link for the actions it names, which moves those statements' values directly. Whether the movement reproduces the study's promotion in the cases the study had in mind is a calibration question, recorded in section 4.4 rather than assumed.

### 4.4 Calibration and validation

The labeled material is the gate's 89 reference statements (65 matched in a briefing scope; the 24 matched nowhere are detection gaps and are excluded, as in the study) and the Chicago answer key of issue #308. The baselines to beat are on record: compression gain per character at AUC 0.610 pooled and 0.552 within datasets; action-class membership at no separation.

The test, in order:

1. **Defaults first.** Score the 65 matched statements under the catalog defaults of section 4.2, with reach and effect from the sibling measurement, no fitting of any kind. Report pooled AUC and mean within-dataset AUC against the tiers, and the median ranks by tier. The defaults must do most of the work: the pass bar is within-dataset AUC of at least 0.65 under defaults alone.
2. **Constrained fit.** Fit only the link weights — one parameter per catalog entry, lever and the measured terms frozen — by a pairwise ranking objective (must statements ranked above should statements) on the gated tables' statements. Re-report the same metrics. If fitted weights separate and defaults do not, the conclusion is that the catalog's structure is right and its argued numbers are wrong, and the fitted values become the proposed defaults with their provenance recorded as fitted, not argued.
3. **Held out.** Score the statements of the gate's seven held-out datasets, written before any tuning (the study's protocol), under defaults and under the fitted weights. Pass requires held-out within-dataset AUC under defaults of at least 0.65, and the fitted weights' held-out AUC no more than 0.05 above the defaults' — a larger gap means the fit is memorizing fifteen tables and the defaults are decoration.
4. **Chicago.** On a profile that states the two selection-relevant statements of issue #308 (the fail-rate contrast by `inspection_type` and the risk-class null, both specify-family), both must outrank every bookkeeping line in the pointed briefing under the pointed profile.
5. **Sensitivity.** Recompute the gated AUCs with every link weight at the low end of its catalog range, then at the high end, and report the spread. Report also the share of statements whose 4,000-character briefing membership changes across the range; the OECD requirement (section 3.6) is that this be measured and published with the score, not that it be zero.

**Falsification.** The two-factor approach, as specified here, is falsified if any of the following holds: defaults score at or below compression's within-dataset AUC (0.552) on the gated tables; held-out AUC under defaults falls at or below compression's pooled baseline (0.610); or briefing membership is unstable across the stated weight ranges, with a third or more of statements changing membership between the low and high ends. Any of the three means the traversal factor, as catalogued, carries no usable signal, and the ranking problem returns to the study's rule with gain per character. Above all of this stands the study's falsification of the cornerstone itself: if agent task tests show no improvement from briefings selected by actionability, actionability is the wrong basis, and this document's number is a precise answer to a wrong question.

### 4.5 Where the number is ugly

Named, not smoothed over:

- **The per-family normalization is an exchange rate.** Expressing each family's effect as a fraction of its own scale asserts that a maximal partition and a maximal specification are worth the same at equal reach, lever, and link. There is no measurement behind that assertion; it is the price of comparing across families at all, placed in one visible location instead of distributed through a weighted sum.
- **Effect and traversal are not independent.** Large effects get acted on more readily than small ones, so link estimated for a typical statement of its family already contains some of the effect's influence, and the product counts part of it twice. The direction of the error is known (it stretches the scale, favoring dramatic statements); its size is not.
- **Reach is a proxy.** The share of the analysis that flows through an object is not the share of decisions that flow through it. A column can carry half the table's measures and none of its decisions. Declared purpose is the only correction, and bare mode does not have it.
- **The defaults encode one standing guess about other people's organizations.** They will be wrong for many readers, in unknown directions. The override mechanism helps exactly the readers who already know their own linkage — the ones who need the defaults least.
- **The reference decision problem is a fiction.** The generic analyst's next steps are a constructed decision problem, chosen because it is the richest one the library can score against without a declared purpose. The number is defensible only as long as it is presented as conditional on that fiction.
- **The score will be gamed.** Once value is the ranking target, detector work will drift toward statements shaped like high-value ones — large reach, fashionable actions — whether or not they are the statements a table most needs. The gate's held-out statements and the falsification bars are the only standing defense, and they measure the drift only where labels exist.

## 5. What this document does not decide

- **Implementation.** Issue #353 sequences the selection implementation behind both this design and the sibling measurement of per-family effects. The formula's effect slot is designed here; its contents arrive with that measurement, and the calibration of section 4.4 cannot run before it.
- **The escalate rendering gap.** The study recorded that the library states facts that force a question but never states the question. A catalog entry for an escalate action presumes a question exists to act on; producing it is the derived recommendation layer of issue #349, not this document.
- **Whether reach generalizes impact correctly.** Section 4.1 extends QuickInsights' impact from a share of one measure to a share of the analysis. The calibration's first step is also the test of that extension: if defaults fail while fitted weights succeed, the failure cannot be attributed between the reach term and the link weights without a further ablation, which the calibration should then run.
