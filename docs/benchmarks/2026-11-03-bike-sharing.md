## Bike Sharing — new bundled dataset

UCI dataset 275 (DOI 10.24432/C5W894, verified on the UCI dataset
page 2026-11-03; CC BY 4.0; Hadi Fanaee-T, 2013): hourly Capital
Bikeshare (Washington, DC) rental counts for 2011–2012 with the
hour's weather and calendar context — 17,379 rows, 17 columns.
Bundled under `benchmarks/corpus/data/anomaly/bike-sharing-hourly/`
with `prepare.py` and `ATTRIBUTION.md`, in the anomaly pack: a
real-world hourly demand stream alongside the traffic entry, but
without published anomaly labels, so its expectations pin observed
behaviour and documented misses rather than label coverage.

Preparation is extraction only: `prepare.py` verifies the source
zip against a pinned sha256
(`b70182d0d0508e9abbb79306ce5c0cec34869000f8220175ac83d11dbe845401`,
matched by a fresh download on 2026-11-03) and writes the published
`hour.csv` byte for byte (`data.csv` sha256
`e03de4ee4ef4dc376ac6e04bf829673c6269e8eba5c60fa121640fa2f829504f`).
The dataset's quirks are kept as served: `dteday` stays a string,
six calendar columns are derived from it, the hourly grid has 165
missing hours, and `weathersit` = 4 occurs on three rows.

## Provenance of the expectations

The dataset was profiled as an unseen case study on 2026-11-03,
before bundling (pass 1, frame exactly as published, standard
budget: 100 findings — the report cap — in 39.2 s; the bundled
corpus run reproduces the profile: 100 findings, 12/12
expectations passing, ~25 s). Every quantitative claim below was
also computed independently of the profiler with Polars during
that study.

Expectations pinned:

- Exact functional dependencies `(cnt, casual) -> registered` and
  `(registered, casual) -> cnt` (0 violations in 17,379 rows;
  verified independently by group counting).
- `instant` uniquely identifies rows (the published row counter,
  exactly 1..17,379 in row order).
- Value concentration: `holiday` is 0 on 97.12% of rows (report:
  97.1%).
- A `max_findings` guard of 100 that records a saturation fact,
  not headroom: the first run selected 100 of 140 candidates, so
  the guard trips when the cap or the selection widens without
  this entry being re-baselined.

Seven documented cases, all status `missed`, recorded for hand
review rather than as finding expectations:

- **The additive identity.** `cnt = casual + registered` on all
  17,379 rows, zero exceptions — the dataset's defining
  arithmetic — is stated by no finding: `arithmetic_identities`
  coverage reads "11 candidate pairs checked, 0 identities" and
  the multiterm screen finds none. The three pairwise FDs (two
  pinned above) are what the report offers instead.
- **The `dteday -> holiday` dependency.** The FD holds exactly
  (731 dates, none carrying more than one holiday value) and was
  originally pinned as a finding expectation. On the frame-model
  build (PR #102, merged after this entry was first prepared) it
  is generated by the dependencies pass but not selected: the
  report saturates at the 100-finding cap under cap pressure
  (196 candidates), and the six `dteday` -> calendar FDs fall
  out of the selected set. Detection is unchanged — the same 11
  exact FDs are found on both builds. Demoted to a documented
  case on 2026-11-03; see the case study's frame-build
  comparison.
- **Hourly grid gaps.** 165 of 17,544 hourly slots in the span
  have no row; 76 of 731 days are short. 2012-10-29 has one row
  and 2012-10-30 eleven (Hurricane Sandy). No statement of span
  or completeness; breaks and ordered_partitions decline because
  `dteday` loads as a string.
- **The composite key.** (`dteday`, `hr`) is unique — 17,379
  distinct pairs — but no finding states it: keys coverage reads
  "1 single-column unique, 0 composite" (the composite screen
  stands down when a single-column key exists) and the headline
  names only the row counter.
- **The season boundary.** `season` is a function of the date,
  not of the month: it flips at a day boundary inside months 3,
  6, 9, 12 (2011-03-21, 2011-06-21, 2011-09-23, 2011-12-21; the
  same dates in 2012). No finding states the boundary; the
  month-based conditional rules cover only the ranges where the
  month reading happens to hold.
- **`weathersit` = 4 on three rows** (distribution 11,413 /
  4,544 / 1,419 / 3). The heaviest documented weather category
  is nearly absent; no finding isolates it.
- **`windspeed` exactly 0 on 12.54% of rows** (2,179 rows), a
  floor pattern in a normalized measure; the sentinels scan
  reported nothing.

The first four cases are the frame-model cases this entry
exists to hold — an identity the profiler should state as an
identity, a generated dependency the selection cap should not
silently drop, completeness against an assembled time grid, and
the near-key statement next to the row counter. The last three
are dataset facts the current detector set has no statement
for.
